Scale-separated Quasi-dS and the Swampland

with Ivano Basile, Shi Chen and Cumrun Vafa

Abstract

Ideas from the Swampland program (dS conjecture and TCC) suggest that it is impossible to obtain solutions of string theory/M-theory in the weak-coupling regions describing our current universe, with V>0V>0V>0, ∥∇V∥/V<2/d−2\lVert\nabla V\rVert/V < 2/\sqrt{d-2}∥∇V∥/V<2/d−2​ and V≪m2V \ll m^2V≪m2 (scale-separated “quasi-dS”) where mmm is the mass of the light tower. We test this expectation in M-theory compactifications and find that even after including the quantum effect of Casimir energy, for hyperbolic and (rigid) Riemann-flat manifolds, de Sitter critical points under EFT control are excluded: the slope of the potential cannot drop below the asymptotic value predicted by the TCC ∥∇V∥/V≳2/d−2\lVert\nabla V\rVert/V\gtrsim 2/\sqrt{d-2}∥∇V∥/V≳2/d−2​ except perhaps in an order-one region in the bulk of moduli space, where EFT control is lost. At the parametric level, our results hold at one-loop level in supergravity; using the scalar-field Casimir energy as a proxy, we also obtain explicit numerical bounds to provide a ballpark estimate of how much control one can expect to achieve in such settings.
