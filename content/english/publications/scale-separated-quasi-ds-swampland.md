#### Abstract

Ideas from the Swampland program (dS conjecture and TCC) suggest that it is impossible to obtain solutions of string theory/M-theory in the weak-coupling regions describing our current universe, with \\(V>0\\), \\(\lVert\nabla V\rVert/V < 2/\sqrt{d-2}\\) and \\(V \ll m^2\\) (scale-separated "quasi-dS") where \\(m\\) is the mass of the light tower. We test this expectation in M-theory compactifications and find that even after including the quantum effect of Casimir energy, for hyperbolic and (rigid) Riemann-flat manifolds, de Sitter critical points under EFT control are excluded: the slope of the potential cannot drop below the asymptotic value predicted by the TCC \\(\lVert\nabla V\rVert/V\gtrsim 2/\sqrt{d-2}\\) except perhaps in an order-one region in the bulk of moduli space, where EFT control is lost. At the parametric level, our results hold at one-loop level in supergravity; using the scalar-field Casimir energy as a proxy, we also obtain explicit numerical bounds to provide a ballpark estimate of how much control one can expect to achieve in such settings.
